# Acme Digital Writing Style Guide
Version 1.5 — August 2025

---

## Style reference order
When in doubt, check in this order: this guide first, then AP Stylebook, then Webster's New World College Dictionary.

American English is the default style for all content.

---

## Writing best practices

### Avoid clichés and jargon
Replace overused jargon with clearer alternatives:

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| transformation | reinvention, evolution |
| optimize | improve, enhance, hone |
| robust | powerful, impressive, potent |
| uplevel | raise, elevate, improve |
| leverage / utilize | use, tap into |
| drive | propel, push, spur, encourage |
| deep dive | in-depth view |

### Less is more
Replace wordy phrases with simpler alternatives:

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| at the present time / at this point in time | now |
| because of the fact that / due to the fact that | because |
| are of the opinion that | believe |
| are able to / have the ability to | can |
| in spite of the fact that | although; despite |
| prior to | before |
| concerning the matter of | about |
| a large number of | many |
| we're in need of | need |
| safe and secure | safe; secure |
| best of breed | best |
| leverage; utilize | use |

### Writing about companies
Honor a company's branding — capitalize names the way the company does (iPhone, eBay). When referring to a company or team, use "it," not "they." (The company changed its name.)

## Punctuation

### Ampersands
Do not use in body copy. Acceptable when space is limited: social media, navigation tabs, graphs, event signage.

### Colons
Capitalize the first word after a colon only when it's a proper noun or begins a complete sentence — not when a fragment follows.

### Commas (serial/Oxford)
Always use the serial comma before the conjunction in a list of three or more items.
**Do:** "The flag was red, white, and blue."

### Dashes
- **Em dash (—):** Used for abrupt change in thought, lengthy pause, or introducing a definition. No spaces before or after.
- **En dash (–):** Used for ranges of numbers or dates. No spaces on either side. (April 1–Aug. 15)
- **Hyphen (-):** Used to join compound modifiers. No spaces. (full-time job, small-business owner)

### Ellipses
Used to show a pause or break in thought, or to indicate omitted words. No space before or after.

### Exclamation points
Avoid. They make copy look unprofessional.

### Periods
One space after a period.

### Quotes
Commas and periods go inside the closing quotation mark. Use present tense when attributing quotes, except when referring to past events.

### Slashes
No space before or after a slash.

## Headlines and subheads

### Headlines
- Use sentence case: capitalize only the first word and proper nouns
- No period at the end of short, clear headlines
- Maximum 70 characters including spaces
- Shorter headlines perform better, especially on mobile

### Subheads
- Sentence case with no ending punctuation (unless a question)
- Maximum 50 characters including spaces
- Do not hyperlink subheads

### When title case is acceptable
- Event agendas (breakouts, workshops, labs)
- On-site venue signage
- References to externally published works
- Navigation labels and calls to action

### Call to action (CTA)
Use sentence case wording with no end punctuation.

## Numbers and dates

### Numbers
Spell out one through nine. Use numerals for 10 and above. Include commas in numerals over 999 (1,000). Do not start a sentence with a numeral.

### Dates
Abbreviate long month names when they include a day (Nov. 18, Jan. 5). Use numerals without ordinals (Oct. 1, not Oct. 1st). Spell out months when they appear alone or with a year only (August 2025).

### Percentages
Use numerals and the % symbol (7%). For ranges: a 12% to 15% increase.

### Dollars
Use numerals and the $ symbol ($3, $125,000). Use words for million and billion ($7 million). Use decimals for non-exact amounts ($12.35 million). In space-limited contexts, use M or B with no space ($8M, $17.3B).

### Times
Use numerals. Include periods in a.m. and p.m. (6 a.m., 4:30 p.m.).

## Capitalization

### Sentence case (default)
Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns in all headings, subheads, and body copy.

**Do:** "The future of work starts here"
**Don't:** "The Future Of Work Starts Here"

### Term capitalization
- Capitalize proper nouns that uniquely identify specific people, places, and things
- Capitalize product and application names
- Do not capitalize product features
- Do not capitalize for emphasis (no vanity capitalization) — this includes terms like security, customer service, management

### Title capitalization (people)
Capitalize a person's title only when it immediately precedes their name. Otherwise lowercase.

**Do:** "They introduced Chief Talent Officer Misty Schmidt."
**Do:** "Misty Schmidt is the chief talent officer."

Do not capitalize: business units, department names, workflows, board names.

### Text emphasis
Avoid all caps, bold, and underlining in web copy.

## Hyphenation

### Terms that are always hyphenated
- low-code, no-code
- self-service and all self- terms
- off-site, on-site
- decision-maker, decision-making
- consumer-like, problem-solving
- real-time (adj.), back-end (adj.), front-end (adj.), end-user (adj.)

### Terms that are never hyphenated
- *-wide* terms: companywide, enterprisewide, worldwide
- *work-* terms: workflow, workforce, workplace, workspace, workstream, workweek
- as-a-service terms: software as a service, platform as a service (even as direct adjectives). Abbreviations are intercapped: SaaS, PaaS.

### Prefixes that do not require hyphens
after-, anti-, bi-, counter-, extra-, inter-, intra-, macro-, mega-, meta-, micro-, mid-, mini-, multi-, non-, over-, post-, pre-, re-, semi-, socio-, sub-, super-, ultra-, un-, under-

## Bullet points
- Introduce with a colon and precede with a blank line
- Capitalize the first word of each bullet
- Include punctuation for complete sentences
- Use consistent grammatical structure within the list (all phrases or all sentences)
- Aim for 2–4 bullets per list
- Use 1., 2. for numbered lists — not 1), 2)

---

## Links
Anchor text should be no more than six words. Do not use "Click here." Use searchable words for internal links. Use the company name for external links. Always end content with a call to action and link.

URLs should be all lowercase unless beginning a sentence.
