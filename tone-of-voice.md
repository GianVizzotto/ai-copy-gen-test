# Acme Tone of Voice

## Brand Voice Principles

These pairs define our voice. Always honor both sides of the balance — never lean too far in either direction.

| We are... | We are not... |
|---|---|
| Friendly | Folksy |
| Understanding | Intimate |
| Genuine | Unrefined |
| Conversational | Wordy |
| Hopeful | Idealistic |
| Bright | Naïve |
| Fearless | Foolish |
| Humble | Timid |
| Bold | Reckless |

---

## Writing Rules

### Keep it simple
Avoid jargon. Our category can be complex, but our audience are ultimately just people. Use everyday words and shorter sentences wherever possible. Limit reliance on acronyms — spell things out when in doubt.

**Do:** "Fix issues faster with automated workflows."
**Don't:** "Leverage ITSM-integrated ML pipelines to optimize MTTR."

---

### Write like you speak
Think of it as a great conversation. How would you talk to a friend? It's a give and take. Be sensitive to your audience, their needs and desires. You have interesting things to say — but it's never one-sided.

---

### Lead with empathy
Always put yourself in the shoes of the audience. They don't owe us their interest — we have to earn it, line by line. Demonstrate empathy for their challenges and struggles. When we truly understand their needs, we know how to solve for them. Those solutions and benefits should be the focus of every message.

---

### Use active voice
In the active voice, the subject of the sentence owns the action. It is the more assertive, forward way to speak.

**Do (active):** "The application connects people."
**Don't (passive):** "People are connected with the application."

---

### Focus on the benefit
As you describe a subject or feature, never lose sight of the emotional benefit to the customer. What we say should never stray far from what matters most to them.

**Do:** "Automate mundane tasks with machine learning and focus on the work that matters most."
**Don't:** "Our machine learning module processes repetitive task queues automatically."

---

### Use first-person plural for Acme, second-person for the audience
- Refer to Acme as **"we"** and **"us"**
- Refer to the audience as **"you"**

**Do:** "We help you move faster."
**Don't:** "Acme helps customers move faster."

---

### Avoid clichés
Clever is expressing an idea in a unique or engaging way. Cliché is expressing it with trite phrases, mundane wordplay, or puns. Occasional humor is acceptable — but cleverness should never get in the way of the message. If you're not sure, find a better way.

---

## Style Guidelines

### AP Style
We use the Associated Press Stylebook as the basis for style. Unless otherwise noted, look to AP for general guidance.

### Sentence case for headlines and subheads
Capitalize only the first word in headlines, subheads, and bulleted lists. Capitalize proper nouns and brand names as always.

**Do:** "The future of work starts here"
**Don't:** "The Future Of Work Starts Here"

> Exception: When a headline is placed on top of a background image or texture, use ALL CAPS per the anthemic graphic treatment.

### Serial comma (Oxford Comma)
Always use the serial comma to separate items in a list, no matter how long or short.

**Do:** "The flag was red, white, and blue."
**Don't:** "The flag was red, white and blue."
